2. Where the area is going

Recent and planned investment (dated):

  • Aug 13, 2026: the state housing agency (WHEDA) awarded a $1.25 million Restore Main Street loan to Historic Mitchell Street Residences at 1101-1113 W. Historic Mitchell St. That is a $9.5 million rehab adding 25 apartments over ground-floor commercial and community tenants (a child development center and a community health center), backed by a $480,000 city TIF. Construction is expected to finish in late 2027. The same round awarded $622,545 to convert the upper floors of the 1927 former JCPenney building at 1125 W. Historic Mitchell St. into 16 apartments, keeping first-floor commercial space13.
  • Jul 7, 2026: the City committed financial support to rehabilitate the long-vacant former Grand department store, a 47,000+ sq ft Art Deco landmark on Mitchell Street, instead of demolishing it14.
  • Oct 12, 2026: a new café, New Halo Cafe, was proposed for a formerly blighted corner at 564 W. Rogers St. in the Historic Mitchell Street neighborhood. The site was bought from the City in 2025, and licenses were pending at the time of the report15.
  • Ongoing: the Historic Mitchell Street BID's August 20, 2026 board agenda included a 2026 small-business grant for property owners and new corridor banners16.
  • Established public investment: the Mitchell Street TIF district (TID 71, created 2007) was set up to support retail, office and service businesses on the corridor17. The Mitchell Street library and apartment redevelopment opened in 2017-201811. The City's "Historic Mitchell St" Targeted Investment Neighborhood opened January 202512.
  • Planned road work: a WisDOT project is listed for traffic signals and site work at S. 16th St. and S. Muskego Ave., with anticipated letting in March 2028 (details subject to change)18.
  • Street name note: on April 21, 2026 the Common Council voted to rename S. Cesar E. Chavez Drive back to S. 16th Street. A permanent new name is under review by the City's naming committee19.

Safety trend (citywide, stated neutrally): Milwaukee Police Department year-end data for 2025 showed total violent crime down 22%, property crime down 15% and nonfatal shootings down 19% from 2024, while homicides rose 8%5. MPD's second-quarter 2026 report showed homicides and nonfatal shootings each down 28% from the same period in 20256. Corridor stakeholders have also held walks with the area alderman on traffic safety and code compliance7.

What it means for a business opening now: public and private money is going into the Mitchell Street corridor: new apartments, restored historic storefronts and a TIF-backed rehab finishing in 2027. A tenant who signs now gets settled before those projects finish and while the corridor is changing.

Zoning: what is allowed

  • The building is zoned LB2 (Local Business), category Commercial, per City of Milwaukee zoning GIS and the parcel (MPROP) record2021. The City describes LB2 as a more urban local-business district with smaller lots and setbacks than LB1, meant to serve neighborhood customers.
  • The City's commercial-district use table (Milwaukee Code ch. 295, subchapter 6, Table 295-603-1)22 lists each use as Y (permitted), L (limited use: allowed if it meets set standards) or S (special use: needs Board of Zoning Appeals approval). In local business districts, general office, medical office, financial services and personal services are core uses. Uses such as health clinics, day care, social service facilities, schools, currency exchanges/payday lenders and body-art studios can carry "L" or "S" conditions.
  • Before signing, each tenant should confirm its specific use with the City of Milwaukee Development Center (zoning review). Gamino Real Estate will help with that check.
  • The parcel is not in a local historic district or a zoning overlay. It sits about 0.3-0.5 mile from the Mitchell Street TIF district and the BID 4 and BID 38 boundaries12.
